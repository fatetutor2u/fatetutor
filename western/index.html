Western Astrology: Your Birth Chart

Western astrology reads the sky at birth by the twelve signs of the zodiac and twelve houses. This page sets out your chart by the oldest rules that are free to quote: Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos (2nd century, in an 1822 translation), with houses and orbs from two early-1900s handbooks. It shows the chart, not a reading of it.

How the chart is set, in the books' words

Ptolemy's aspects are whole fractions of the circle: opposition (180°), trine (120°), square (90°) and sextile (60°):

or four signs, or a hundred and twenty degrees

or two signs, or sixty degrees

How close counts (the orb), from Alan Leo:

For conjunction or opposition allow 12° when the Sun aspects the Moon

For square and trine about 8° all round may be allowed.

Each planet's own signs (its houses), from Ptolemy:

Leo for the Sun, as being masculine; and Cancer for the Moon

his exaltation, therefore, is determined to be in Aries

The houses come from the midheaven through a table of houses, as Sepharial shows:

This will give the approximate Right Ascension of the Midheaven at birth.

Quoted as printed: Ptolemy, Tetrabiblos (tr. J. M. Ashmand, 1822); Alan Leo, How to Judge a Nativity; Sepharial, Astrology: How to Make and Read Your Own Horoscope. All public domain.

    What this page leaves out, and why

    What the positions mean: the books call some aspects good and others bad, and use the chart to time events. Those are judgements and forecasts, so they are not shown.

    Pluto (found in 1930, after these books), the Moon's nodes, and the minor aspects that Leo adds (semi-square, quincunx and others): none of the four books gives them a rule this page can quote, so they are left out.

    Ashmand translated Proclus' later Greek paraphrase of Ptolemy, not Ptolemy's own text; it is the oldest full English version that is free to quote. Leo himself calls his orbs guidance: In this, experience can be the only real teacher

    Where this comes from

    Ptolemy, Alan Leo and Sepharial

    The aspects and the planets' own signs come from Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos (Book I, chapters 16, 20 and 22, tr. Ashmand 1822); the orbs from Alan Leo's How to Judge a Nativity; the houses from Sepharial's handbook and its table of houses for London. All are public domain, read from the Internet Archive.

    Checked: the houses agree with all 372 rows of Sepharial's printed table (three printing slips are named in our checker); the Sun, Moon and planets agree with an independent ephemeris within seconds of arc.

    About this tradition

    Astrology of the Greek and Latin world

    Western astrology took its classic shape in Hellenistic Egypt. Ptolemy of Alexandria, who also wrote the Almagest, set out its rules in the Tetrabiblos in the 2nd century; English handbooks carried them into modern times.

    Fate Tutor sets out the chart and shows its sources. It does not turn a chart into a forecast, financial advice, medical advice or a decision made for you.

    These are traditional systems of belief. They are not science and they do not predict the future. Fate Tutor shows what each tradition says and where it says it, in the language of tendencies.
