Plum Blossom Numbers: From a Moment to a Hexagram

This method, linked with Shao Yong, turns the date and hour, or anything you can count, into a hexagram. Every step of the arithmetic is shown, and the reading stays with what the classical text says.

The date becomes a lunar date. Year branch, month and day go above; add the hour for below and for the moving line.

The date also gives the lunar month, used for season strength. The hour is a two-hour period: 子 is 23:00–00:59, 丑 is 01:00–02:59, and so on.

Everything is worked out on this device. Nothing is sent to us.

The rule, as the book states it

「年月日為上卦。年月日加時總數為下卦。」

「如得八數整，即坤卦」

「凡起動爻，以重卦總數除六，以餘數作動爻。」

「取爻當以時加之。」

Quoted from 梅花易數 卷一, public domain.

    體 (body) is the trigram without the moving line: it stands for you, the one asking. 用 (use) holds the moving line: it stands for the matter asked about.

    Where this comes from

    梅花易數, attributed to Shao Yong (邵雍)

    The rules and quotes come from the 梅花易數 text on Chinese Wikisource, which is public domain. Each rule was also checked against three printed editions, and they agree.

    Two points are shown openly. The sound method does not state its own moving-line rule, so the book's general rule (add the hour) is used. The book judges season strength by month and day; this page uses the month only.

    About this tradition

    Numbers from a moment, read through body and use

    Plum Blossom Numbers is a method linked with the Song-dynasty scholar Shao Yong. It builds a hexagram from numbers and reads it mainly through the five elements of the 體 and 用 trigrams.

    Fate Tutor shows the method and its source. It does not turn a cast into a forecast, financial advice, medical advice or a decision made for you.

    These are traditional systems of belief. They are not science and they do not predict the future. Fate Tutor shows what each tradition says and where it says it, in the language of tendencies.
