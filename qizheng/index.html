Seven Governors and Four Remainders: Your Birth Sky

Chinese star astrology reads the Sun, Moon and five planets (七政) with four computed points (四餘) against twelve palaces and 28 lunar mansions. This page sets them out by the Qing court calendar (時憲曆) as 清史稿 records it: the real sky, and the court's own rules for the four remainders.

How the sky is set, in the books' words

The twelve palaces are 30° each, starting at the winter solstice:

「初度為冬至，十五度為小寒」

Each mansion starts at its marker star, moved on by the slow drift of the stars:

「以積年乘歲差，得數加甲子法元黃道宿度，為本年宿鈐」

The four remainders, as the court calendar computes them:

「月孛用最高實行，羅㬋用正交實行加減六宮，計都用正交實行」

紫氣 goes round once in about 28 years:

「約二十八年十閏而氣行一周天」

The life palace: the Sun's palace, counted from the birth hour round to 卯:

「星家既以太陽數至卯為命」

Quoted as printed: 清史稿 (1928) and 星學大成 (1563), public domain on Chinese Wikisource.

How it was worked out

    Reading the sky

    The palaces follow the Sun's year (they are the same twelve sections as the Western signs). The mansions follow the stars, so they slowly slide against the palaces: today they sit about 4° further on than in 1744.

    The Sun, Moon and planets are where astronomy puts them. 月孛, 羅㬋 and 計都 are the Qing court's corrected lunar apogee and nodes; 紫氣 has no body in the sky and follows its own fixed rate.

    These are the facts the tradition reads from. Fate Tutor does not turn them into a forecast.

    What this page leaves out, and why

    The transforming stars (化曜), the many spirit stars (神煞), the periods of life (限度) and the books' readings are not shown: they turn the sky into judgements, and some touch topics Fate Tutor does not cover.

    The older Ming handbooks (張果星宗, 星學大成) print fixed tables of their own era; this page follows the later Qing court method instead. 清史稿 records how the remainders were defined and renamed: 「羅睺即白道之正交，計都即中交，月孛乃月行極高之點」 「是年更定羅睺、計都名目，又增入紫炁為四餘」

    Twelve of the 28 marker stars are fixed by 清史稿's own 1744 positions, and the catalogue matches them within 3′. For 心 the page uses the mansion's first star (σ Sco); some modern lists use Antares instead. 「以參宿中三星之東一星作距星」

    Where this comes from

    清史稿, 星學大成 and the Yale Bright Star Catalogue

    The palaces, the mansion method and the four remainders come from the calendar treatise of 清史稿 (chapters 45, 48, 50) and its astronomy chapter (28). The life palace and the twelve palace roles come from 星學大成 (萬民英, 1563). The 28 marker stars come from the Yale Bright Star Catalogue. All are public domain.

    Checked: the stars carried back to 1744 match 清史稿's printed positions within 3′; the planets agree with an independent ephemeris within a few arcseconds; the remainders are rebuilt from the constants read out of 清史稿 and agree within a second of arc.

    About this tradition

    Chinese star astrology

    Qi Zheng Si Yu grew from the Tang and Song dynasties and flourished in the Ming. It reads the actual sky at birth, divided by palaces and by the 28 lunar mansions of Chinese astronomy.

    Fate Tutor sets out the sky and shows its sources. It does not turn a chart into a forecast, financial advice, medical advice or a decision made for you.

    These are traditional systems of belief. They are not science and they do not predict the future. Fate Tutor shows what each tradition says and where it says it, in the language of tendencies.
